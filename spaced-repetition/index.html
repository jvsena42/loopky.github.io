What is spaced repetition?

Spaced repetition is a way of studying where you review something right before you would forget it, and a little later each time you remember it. It is the idea behind Anki, SuperMemo and Loopky.

The short version

You forget most of what you learn within days. Every time you successfully recall something, it lasts longer before you forget it again. Spaced repetition uses that: it shows you a card, asks you to recall the answer, and schedules the next review based on how well you did. Easy cards come back rarely; hard cards come back soon. You spend your time on what you are about to lose.

Why it works

  • The forgetting curve. Hermann Ebbinghaus measured in the 1880s how memory fades after learning. Each well timed review flattens the curve.
  • The spacing effect. The same amount of study spread over days beats one long session. Cramming works for tomorrow and fails next month.
  • The testing effect. Pulling an answer out of memory strengthens it far more than rereading it. Flashcards force that retrieval every time.

How Loopky schedules a card

After you reveal the answer, you grade yourself with one of four buttons. Each button shows the exact interval it will give the card, so there are no surprises:

ButtonNext review, by defaultCan you change it?
AgainIn under 10 minutes, same sessionNo
Hard1 dayYes, 1 to 365 days
Good3 daysYes, 1 to 365 days
Easy7 daysYes, 1 to 365 days

Loopky uses fixed intervals: the interval you set for a button is the interval you get, every review. That keeps the schedule predictable and easy to tune, for example shortening everything before an exam. The trade off is that intervals do not grow on their own the way they do in SM-2 or FSRS, so a card you know well keeps returning on its Easy interval. If you want reviews spaced out to months, set Easy to 30 days or more.

New cards are separate from reviews. The default goal is 20 new cards a day, and you can set it anywhere from 1 to 999. Your intervals and goals sync across your devices.

How much to study

For most people, 10 to 20 minutes a day is plenty. Consistency matters more than volume: a short session every day beats a long one on weekends, because the whole method depends on reviewing close to the right day. If you miss a few days, just start again; overdue cards wait for you.

How to write cards you will remember

  1. One fact per card. "Capital of Australia?" is a good card. "Everything about Australia" is not.
  2. Make it a question. Cards that ask something force recall. Cards that state something invite rereading.
  3. Add context. For vocabulary, use a sentence. For concepts, use your own words.
  4. Use pictures. An image is often faster to recall than a definition.
  5. Be honest with the buttons. If you hesitated, it was Hard. Grading generously just means forgetting later.
  6. Understand first, memorise second. Flashcards hold what you already understand. They are not a substitute for learning it.

Questions

What is spaced repetition in simple terms?

It is reviewing information at increasing gaps, timed so you review each item right before you would forget it. Apps like Loopky and Anki do the timing for you.

Is spaced repetition better than rereading notes?

Yes. Research on the spacing and testing effects consistently shows that recalling answers at spaced intervals leads to much better long term memory than rereading or cramming.

How many flashcards should I study a day?

Start with 10 to 20 new cards a day and do all your due reviews. Loopky's default new card goal is 20.

What algorithm does Loopky use?

Fixed intervals per grade button. Again returns the card within 10 minutes; Hard, Good and Easy default to 1, 3 and 7 days and can each be set from 1 to 365 days.
