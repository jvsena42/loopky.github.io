For language learners

Flashcards that talk back, for learning a language

Hear every word, say it out loud, and let spaced repetition bring it back right before you forget. Loopky is a free flashcards app built around the way vocabulary actually sticks.

Reading a word is not the same as knowing it. You need to recognise it when you hear it and produce it when you speak. Loopky gives every language deck three ways to practise, and schedules each card with spaced repetition so a few minutes a day is enough.

Three ways to study a card

  • Listen. The card is read aloud with the phone's own text to speech voice for that language, so you learn the sound with the spelling.
  • Speak. Say the answer out loud. Loopky hands it to your device's speech recognition and checks it. The recording is not stored or uploaded by Loopky.
  • Type. Type the answer and see what you got right. Good for spelling, accents and characters.

35 languages and variants

Each side of a deck can have its own language, so the front can be Japanese and the back English. The language picker offers what your phone can voice, and always includes this common set:

Arabic, Bengali, Chinese (Mainland and Taiwan), Czech, Danish, Dutch, English (UK and US), Finnish, French (France and Canada), German, Greek, Hebrew, Hindi, Hungarian, Indonesian, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Norwegian, Polish, Portuguese (Brazil and Portugal), Romanian, Russian, Slovak, Spanish (Spain and Mexico), Swedish, Thai, Turkish, Ukrainian and Vietnamese.

How natural a voice sounds depends on the voices installed on your phone.

Build a vocabulary deck in a minute

  1. Paste a list. Copy a word list from a textbook, a spreadsheet, or your notes. Loopky detects the separator (tab, comma, semicolon, pipe, colon, a dash, or a markdown table) and makes one card per line.
  2. Turn on reverse cards to practise both directions: target language to yours, and yours to the target language.
  3. Add pictures from Unsplash or your gallery. An image next to a word beats a translation for concrete nouns.

Already have decks in Anki? Import your .apkg files. Want someone to do the typing? Ask your AI to write the deck, for example "the 300 most common Spanish verbs with an example sentence each", or "the vocabulary from the first episode of the show I am watching".

Find decks other learners made

The Discover tab lists decks published by people using Loopky, searchable by topic. Language decks are tagged by language automatically, so finding Japanese or German decks is one tap. Follow a deck, or clone it and edit your own copy.

Tips that make vocabulary stick

  • Put words in a sentence. "el perro ladra" teaches more than "perro".
  • Say it every time. Speak mode feels slow for a day and then becomes the part you remember best.
  • Keep new cards small. The default goal is 20 new cards a day. Fewer, every day, beats a big batch once a week.
  • Kanji and Hanzi: keep one character or one word per card, with the reading on the back and an example word or sentence.

Questions

Can Loopky read flashcards out loud?

Yes. Language decks use the phone's built in text to speech voices in 35 languages and regional variants.

Does Loopky check my pronunciation?

Yes. Speak mode passes what you say to your device's speech recognition and grades the card from the recognised text. Loopky does not store or upload the recording.

Is it good for Japanese or Chinese?

Yes. Japanese, Mandarin (Mainland and Taiwan) and Korean are supported for listening and speaking, and Type mode works with your phone's keyboard for kana and characters.

How is Loopky different from Duolingo?

Duolingo teaches a fixed course. Loopky is a flashcards app: you choose or build the decks, and spaced repetition decides when each card comes back. Many learners use a course and flashcards together.

Is it free?

Yes. Free, no ads, and open source.
